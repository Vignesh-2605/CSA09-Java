REPRODUCING "TOO MANY OPEN FILES" UNDER A LOW DESCRIPTOR LIMIT
Name: Vignesh S   Register Number: 192325002
Date: 2026-11-18 07:37 UTC

The retail chain's report generator fails with "Too many open files".
A single leaked handle never shows that symptom; the leak has to
accumulate.  Here the leaking (manual) version is run over and over in
one JVM, with the leaked readers kept reachable, under a deliberately
low per-process descriptor limit so the limit is reached in seconds.

$ ulimit -n 200
$ java -cp . LeakUnderLimit

  descriptor limit visible to this JVM: Max open files 200 200 files
  after   20 report runs: 20 store handles leaked and still held
  after   40 report runs: 40 store handles leaked and still held
  after   60 report runs: 60 store handles leaked and still held
  after   80 report runs: 80 store handles leaked and still held
  after  100 report runs: 100 store handles leaked and still held
  after  120 report runs: 120 store handles leaked and still held
  after  140 report runs: 140 store handles leaked and still held
  after  160 report runs: 160 store handles leaked and still held
  after  180 report runs: 180 store handles leaked and still held
  STOPPED after 195 report runs
  leaked store handles held at that point: 195
  what stopped it: java.io.FileNotFoundException: stores/store01.csv (Too many open files)

$ ulimit -n 1024        # the same program, a higher limit

  after  980 report runs: 980 store handles leaked and still held
  after 1000 report runs: 1000 store handles leaked and still held
  STOPPED after 1019 report runs
  leaked store handles held at that point: 1019
  what stopped it: java.io.FileNotFoundException: stores/store01.csv (Too many open files)

READ THE TWO RUNS TOGETHER
--------------------------
The program, the input and the bug are identical.  Only the descriptor
limit changed, and the number of reports that succeed changed with it.
That is why the production symptom looked intermittent: it is not the
data that decides when the crash arrives, it is how many handles the
process is allowed and how many it has already leaked.

A NOTE ON THIS HARNESS
----------------------
The first version of this program reported no crash at all.  The
corrupt-row failure and the descriptor exhaustion both surface as
IOException, and one catch clause was absorbing both - so the harness
hid the symptom it existed to show.  The two are now separated by
walking getCause() for "Too many open files".  This is the same
lesson as section 9 of Q1: catch (Exception e) over a whole operation
loses the distinction between faults that mean different things.

The try-with-resources version leaks nothing, so the loop runs to
completion at any descriptor limit.
