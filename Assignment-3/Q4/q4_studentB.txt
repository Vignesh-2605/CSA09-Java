2.  THREAD PRIORITIES  -  DOES THE ORDER CHANGE?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Three conditions, 20 runs each.  The third exists because the first
  two cannot be read on their own: stations are STARTED in the order
  1,2,3,4, so Station-1 has a head start whatever its priority.  Without
  a control, a high-priority Station-1 finishing first proves nothing.

  CONDITION                                  | 1 first  | 2 first  | 3 first  | 4 first  | DISTINCT ORDERS
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CONTROL: all stations priority 5 (default) | 10/20    | 8/20     | 2/20     | 0/20     | 11 of 20
  Station-1 MAX(10), 7, 4, Station-4 MIN(1)  | 7/20     | 8/20     | 3/20     | 2/20     | 10 of 20
  Station-1 MIN(1), 4, 7, Station-4 MAX(10)  | 6/20     | 11/20    | 1/20     | 2/20     | 12 of 20

  HOW TO READ THIS TABLE
      Station-1 finishes first 50% of the time with NO priority advantage,
      35% with MAX priority, and 30% with MIN priority.
      Station-4 finishes first 0% with no advantage and 10% with MAX
      priority - the condition under which it should dominate.

      Station-4 is started last, so it finishes first only rarely in
      every condition, including the one where it has the highest
      priority in the program.  The variable that predicts the finishing
      order here is START ORDER, not priority.

  CONCLUSION ON PRIORITIES
      setPriority() is a HINT to the operating system's scheduler, not an
      instruction to the JVM.  On Linux a plain user process cannot raise
      a thread's OS priority at all, so the hint is largely discarded.
      The table above shows no condition in which the highest-priority
      station reliably finishes first.

      It would not help even if it worked here: these stations spend
      almost all their time in TIMED_WAITING, so they are not competing
      for the processor in the first place.  Priority only influences who
      gets a core when more threads are RUNNABLE than there are cores.
      With 4 stations and 2 processors, there is no queue to jump.
