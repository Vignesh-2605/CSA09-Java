TASK 6  -  INHERITANCE HIERARCHY
================================================================================

1.  THE HIERARCHY AND THE KIND OF INHERITANCE USED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
     Vehicle  (abstract superclass)
        |
        +-- TwoWheeler                  <- single inheritance
        |
        +-- Car                         <- single inheritance
              |
              +-- ElectricCar           <- MULTILEVEL (3 levels)

     Vehicle has TWO direct subclasses, which makes the vehicle side
     HIERARCHICAL as well; ElectricCar extends Car rather than Vehicle,
     which makes that branch MULTILEVEL.  Java has no multiple class
     inheritance, so a capability such as Chargeable is an INTERFACE
     (Task 9), not a second superclass.

     CampusUser  (abstract superclass)
        +-- Student   +-- Staff   +-- Visitor    <- HIERARCHICAL

2.  WHAT IS INHERITED AND WHAT EACH SUBCLASS ADDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CLASS          | INHERITS                   | ADDS               | OVERRIDES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Vehicle        | -                          | regNo, wheels      | -
TwoWheeler     | regNo, wheels, describe()  | -                  | ratePerHour()
Car            | regNo, wheels              | seats              | ratePerHour(), describe()
ElectricCar    | regNo, wheels, seats, rate | batteryKWh         | describe()

  ElectricCar deliberately does NOT override ratePerHour(): an EV is
  parked at the car tariff, and the charging charge is separate (Task 9).
  Inheriting the rate is the design statement that an EV IS-A car for
  billing purposes.

3.  CONSTRUCTOR EXECUTION ORDER WHEN AN ElectricCar IS CREATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  source:  new ElectricCar("TN03EF3333", 40)

  TRACE (printed by the constructors themselves, in the order they ran):
        1. ElectricCar(String,int) body entered, super(regNo,5) called first
        2. Car(String,int) body entered, super(regNo,4) called first
        3. Vehicle(String,int)      regNo=TN03EF3333, wheels=4
        4. Car(String,int)          seats=5
        5. ElectricCar(String,int)  battery=40 kWh

  Reading the printed lines 3, 4, 5 top to bottom gives the rule: the
  MOST GENERAL constructor finishes FIRST.  Each subclass constructor
  suspends at its super(...) call, lets the parent complete, and only
  then runs its own body - so a subclass can never read a field the
  superclass has not yet initialised.

  Formally:  Vehicle(...)  ->  Car(...)  ->  ElectricCar(...)
  and if a constructor omits super(...), the compiler inserts an
  implicit no-argument super() as its first statement.

4.  super USED THREE WAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  on a CONSTRUCTOR : super(regNo, 4) in Car - passes work upward
  on a METHOD      : super.describe() in Car - extends, does not replace
  on a FIELD       : super.wheels in ElectricCar - reads the inherited field

  result: 4-wheeler TN03EF3333, 5 seats, electric 40 kWh (wheels field read through super: 4)

6.  THE USER HIERARCHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NAME           | ROLE       | FEE PAYABLE
Vignesh S      | Student    | no, valid pass
Arun           | Student    | yes, full fee
Dr Ramesh      | Staff      | no, valid pass
Guest          | Visitor    | yes, full fee

  Visitor overrides paysFee() to return true unconditionally, which is
  why 'has a pass' is a field of Student and Staff but not of Visitor -
  a subclass adds only the state its own rule actually needs.
