Q6  -  STARVATION, FAIRNESS AND THE DEPLOYMENT DECISION
======================================================================================================================
Name: Vignesh S   Register Number: 192325002

1.  WHY A SEVERITY-5 PATIENT CAN WAIT INDEFINITELY
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The comparator orders on severity FIRST and arrival only as a
  tie-break.  So a severity-5 case is overtaken by EVERY severity 1-4
  patient, no matter how long it has waited and no matter how recently
  they arrived.  Waiting time has no influence on the ordering at all.

  Formally: the queue serves min(severity, arrival) lexicographically.
  A severity-5 case reaches the head only when the queue contains no
  case of severity 1-4.  If such cases arrive at least as fast as they
  are served, that condition is never reached and the wait is unbounded.
  This is STARVATION, and it is a property of the ORDERING, not a bug in
  the implementation - the heap is doing exactly what it was told.

  Simulation: 20000 ticks, 1.15 arrivals per tick, 1 patient served per tick,
  severity mix 8%/16%/28%/26%/22% for S1..S5.

3.  THE FAIR RULE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DESIGN: a case waiting longer than 120 ticks is promoted by one severity
  level for ordering purposes, and may be promoted repeatedly, but never
  above level 1.  The CLINICAL severity recorded on the chart is never
  altered - only the queue's view of it.

  effectiveSeverity(c, now) = max(1, c.severity - (now - c.arrival) / 120)

  WHY AGING RATHER THAN THE OBVIOUS ALTERNATIVES:
    - a fixed quota ('serve one S5 every 10 patients') is simple but
      serves a minor case ahead of a critical one on a fixed schedule,
      which is clinically indefensible;
    - a pure deadline ('nobody waits over 2 hours') has the same flaw at
      the deadline and produces a stampede when many expire together;
    - aging degrades GRADUALLY: a long-waiting S5 first competes with S4,
      then S3, and reaches the head only if it keeps being overtaken. A
      genuinely critical arrival still goes first, because it enters at
      level 1 and nothing can be promoted above that.

  THE COST: the effective priority depends on 'now', so the heap's
  ordering is no longer stable over time - a key changes while the
  element is inside the heap, which is exactly the mutation hazard Q3
  examined for HashSet.  The implementation therefore recomputes the
  order at service time rather than relying on stale heap positions.

4.  THE FIRST VERSION OF THIS RULE WAS WRONG, AND THE DATA SAID SO
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  My first design promoted without any floor: max(1, severity - waited/T).
  It fixed starvation, and broke something far more important.

MEASURE                          | STRICT             | AGING, NO FLOOR    | VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S5 mean wait                     | 5878.9             | 1318.7             | improved
S5 never served                  | 2965.0             | 672.0              | improved
S1 mean wait                     | 0.0                | 1268.8             | MADE WORSE
S1 worst wait                    | 1.0                | 2583.0             | MADE WORSE

  Starvation of minor cases improved sharply - and CRITICAL patients went
  from a mean wait of 0.0 ticks to 1268.8.

  The cause is structural, not a tuning error.  With no floor, EVERY
  waiting case eventually reaches level 1, so after enough time all cases
  share the same effective severity and the tie-break - arrival order -
  decides everything.  The rule degenerates to FIFO, which is precisely
  the behaviour section 1 showed to be unacceptable.  Lengthening the
  threshold only delays that collapse; it does not prevent it.

  CORRECTION: a promotion FLOOR of 2. A case may age up to level 2 but
  never to level 1, so a genuine severity-1 arrival is never overtaken by
  an aged minor case.  Starvation is bounded; clinical priority is not
  surrendered.

  FAIR with aging + floor 2: 22,965 arrivals, 20,000 served, 2,965 still waiting

  SEVERITY   | ARRIVED    | SERVED     | MEAN WAIT      | WORST WAIT     | NEVER SERVED
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  S1         | 1,844      | 1,844      | 0.0            | 1              | 0
  S2         | 3,657      | 3,150      | 1368.8         | 2,820          | 507
  S3         | 6,410      | 5,525      | 1398.7         | 2,820          | 885

patients served                | 20000.0         | 20000.0           | 20000.0           | STRICT

  The floored rule is the only one of the three that is acceptable on
  BOTH measures: critical patients keep an essentially immediate wait,
  and minor patients are no longer left unseen indefinitely.

  Whether the remaining S5 wait is clinically acceptable is a decision
  for the department, not for the programmer.  The threshold and the
  floor are the two dials that set it, which is why both are named
  constants rather than numbers buried in the comparator.

5.  DEPLOYMENT DECISION: CAN THIS SERVE 5,000 PATIENTS A DAY?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  5,000 patients a day is one arrival every 17.3 seconds on average.
  From the growth measurements in TriageDesign:

    admit() at n = 100,000:                    about 147 ns
    next()  at n = 100,000:                    about 408 ns
    5,000 admissions + 5,000 services:         about 2.8 ms of CPU PER DAY

  The queue is not remotely the bottleneck. A realistic waiting room holds
  tens of patients, not thousands, so n stays small and log n stays under
  6.  The structure would still be comfortable at a hundred times this
  load.

  VERDICT: YES on throughput - but with two caveats that matter more than
  the timings:

    (a) WITHOUT the fair rule it should NOT be deployed at any scale. The
        simulation left 2965 minor patients unseen at the end of the shift
        and the worst minor wait reached 11463 ticks. That is a clinical
        safety failure, and no amount of throughput compensates for it.

    (b) The waiting-list display is O(n log n) because the heap must be
        copied and sorted. On a screen refreshed every few seconds that
        is the dominant cost of the whole system - far larger than admit
        and serve combined. This is the limitation Q7 takes up.
