1.  DO ALL THREE PRODUCE THE SAME RANKING?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A  Comparator chain     : [Yaks(12,0), Zebras(12,0), Eagles(9,6), Bisons(9,4), Panthers(9,4), Antelopes(6,1), Cobras(6,1), Dingoes(6,1)]
  B  Comparable           : [Yaks(12,0), Zebras(12,0), Eagles(9,6), Bisons(9,4), Panthers(9,4), Antelopes(6,1), Cobras(6,1), Dingoes(6,1)]
  C  three stable sorts   : [Yaks(12,0), Zebras(12,0), Eagles(9,6), Bisons(9,4), Panthers(9,4), Antelopes(6,1), Cobras(6,1), Dingoes(6,1)]

  A == C : true
  A == B : true

  WHY C WORKS AT ALL: because List.sort is STABLE - equal elements keep
  their relative order.  Sorting by the LEAST significant key first and
  the MOST significant last means each later sort preserves the ordering
  the earlier ones established among its own ties.  Reverse the order of
  the three calls and the result is simply wrong - the technique depends
  entirely on a property of the sort, which makes it fragile: a future
  change to an unstable sort would break it silently.

3.  COMPLEXITY AND SPACE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
APPROACH                   | SORTS PERFORMED      | TIME                     | EXTRA SPACE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Comparator chain         | 1                    | O(n log n)               | O(n) for the copy + TimSort run stack
B Comparable               | 1                    | O(n log n)               | same as A
C three stable sorts       | 3                    | 3 x O(n log n)           | same, but allocated 3 times

  C is the same complexity class as A and B - a constant multiple of
  three does not change O(n log n) - but it does three times the work,
  and the measurements above show that constant clearly.  A and B do the
  SAME number of comparisons; they differ only in where the comparison
  lives.

4.  WHICH IS EASIEST TO REUSE FOR PLAYERS AND COLLEGES?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
APPROACH                   | SECOND ORDERING OF Team        | NEW TYPE (Player)          | VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Comparator chain         | pass a different Comparator    | same Leaderboard<Player>   | REUSABLE
B Comparable               | impossible without a Comparator | edit Player to implement it | LEAST reusable
C three stable sorts       | rewrite the sequence of sorts  | rewrite again per type     | reusable, but verbose

  A is the answer.  B bakes one ordering into the type, so the programme
  listing (by name) and the statistics page (by goal difference) each
  need a Comparator anyway - at which point the Comparable adds a fourth
  way of ordering the same type and invites the two to disagree.

  B remains worth having for ONE purpose: a natural order lets Team be
  put in a TreeSet or sorted with sort(null) without supplying anything.
  The usual compromise is to implement Comparable with the single most
  obvious order and express every other ordering as a Comparator - and
  for Team the 'most obvious order' is the competition ranking, which is
  exactly the one that changes between competitions. So for THIS type,
  Comparator alone is the cleaner answer.
