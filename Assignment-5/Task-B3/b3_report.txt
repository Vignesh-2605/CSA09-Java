  Statement was not run at 100,000 rows.  At 10,000 it already takes
  long enough to extrapolate honestly, and the shape of the result is
  not in doubt: it is the row-by-row round trip that costs, and
  Statement adds a reparse to every one of them.

  A RESULT THAT DOES NOT MATCH THE USUAL STORY
      The three ROW-BY-ROW rows are indistinguishable from each other.
      At 10,000 records they sit within a few per cent, and across two
      executions of this program their order changed - Statement came
      out ahead of PreparedStatement in one run and behind it in the
      next. The textbook ordering assumes the reparse dominates; here
      it plainly does not, because all three pay one network round trip
      per row and that cost swamps the parse.

      Preparing a statement earns its keep when the statement is
      REUSED, which is what the batch does - and the batch is the only
      row in this table that pulls away as the data grows, from 1.5x at
      1,000 records to 7.8x at 100,000.

      PreparedStatement is still the one to use, but on this evidence
      the reason is SQL injection (Task B2), not speed.

4.  A FAILURE HALFWAY THROUGH  -  AUTO-COMMIT AGAINST ONE TRANSACTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Pass number 500 of 1,000 carries amount_paise = -1, which violates
  the CHECK constraint chk_amount.  The same 1,000 records are loaded
  twice: once with auto-commit on, once inside one transaction.

  MODE                               | WHAT HAPPENED          | ROWS LEFT BEHIND   | VERDICT
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  auto-commit, batches of 100        | BatchUpdateException   | 499                | PARTIAL DATA LEFT IN THE TABLE
  one transaction, batches of 100    | BatchUpdateException   | 0                  | no rows remain - correct

  With auto-commit, 499 rows were already committed before the bad
  record was reached, and they stay in the table. The import is now
  half-done, and running it again would hit duplicate primary keys.

  Inside one transaction, nothing is visible until commit() - and the
  exception means commit() is never reached, so the connection closes
  with the work rolled back. The table is exactly as it was.
